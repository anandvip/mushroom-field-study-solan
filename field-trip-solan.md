# Field-trip plan: Solan, Himachal Pradesh

**A working annex to the Kurukshetra mushroom study.**  
**Focus of the trip:** meetings with institutes and actual growers around Solan, to bring back a baseline dataset that can be compared with Thanesar and Pehowa.  
**Travel window:** after 16 November 2026. Motorcycle from Kurukshetra. Stay of one or two nights at most.  
**Research date for this plan:** 11 November 2026.

> **How to read this plan.** Every contact, date, distance and climate figure has a numbered source [S#]. Anything not checked against a primary or reliable source is marked **Unverified**. Phone numbers and names are copied only from the pages cited; none were invented, and none have been called or emailed for this plan. Prices, training seats and office hours can change, so confirm them before travelling. This is a field plan, not a booking and not an introduction letter.

## 3. Motorcycle route: Kurukshetra to Solan

**Intended line:** Kurukshetra → Ambala → Zirakpur / Panchkula → Parwanoo → NH-5 (old NH-22) via Dharampur / Kumarhatti → Chambaghat / Solan.

**Maps (driving directions to DMR):** [Kurukshetra to ICAR-DMR, Chambaghat](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Kurukshetra,+Haryana/ICAR-Directorate+of+Mushroom+Research,+Chambaghat,+Solan,+Himachal+Pradesh)

**Distance and time (mapping / route aggregators, not a motorcycle survey):**

- Road distance about **146–150 km**; one aggregator that builds a composite car route gives **146 km in 2 hours 23 minutes**, and also states a road distance of 149.6 km and a driving time of about 2 hours 18 minutes [S22].
- That figure is a **car time in clear traffic**. It does not include fuel stops, winter fog on the plains, or a cautious climb after Parwanoo. For a motorcycle in late November, plan **about 4–5 hours door to door, including two short breaks**, and treat the 2-hour-18-minute figure as a lower bound, not a target.
- Confirm the live time in Google Maps on the morning of departure.

**Suggested breaks and fuel (places on the highway, not named pumps):**

| Stretch | Rough role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Kurukshetra | Fill the tank before leaving | Do not start the hills on a low tank |
| Ambala (after the Grand Trunk / NH stretch) | First break, toilets, tea | End of the first plains leg |
| Zirakpur / Panchkula | Second fill if needed | Last easy plains services before the climb |
| Parwanoo | Fuel and a pause before or just after the NH-5 climb starts | A route-safety page for Chandigarh–Shimla (NH-5) lists fuel at Chandigarh, Parwanoo, Solan and Shimla [S23] |

Named retail pumps were not independently verified for this plan, so check Google Maps for an open outlet at each stop on the day.

**Winter hill-riding cautions**

- **Leave after morning fog lifts on the plains, and arrive before dark.** Late-November sunset in Solan is about 5:19–5:33 PM; sunrise is about 6:36–7:00 AM [S11]. Night riding on the hill section is a poor idea.
- IMD’s Solan district note says fog occurs occasionally in the cold season, particularly in the valleys, and that cold waves behind western disturbances can bring frost, with minima occasionally around or just below freezing [S24]. The Ambala–Chandigarh plains can also be foggy on November mornings; that is often the slower part of the day, not the hill itself.
- The Chandigarh–Shimla NH-5 climb is a well-used highway, but a route-safety summary flags heavy weekend and holiday traffic, fog and occasional ice on the upper section in winter, and sharp bends beyond Solan [S23]. The Parwanoo–Solan stretch is still the easier part of that highway; the risk is speed, fog, oil or damp leaves on shaded bends, and other traffic, not high-altitude snow on a typical November day.
- Check the **IMD Shimla forecast and highway weather** the evening before and again at dawn: [IMD Shimla](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/shimla/) and the highway bulletin [web_highway.pdf](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/shimla/mcdata/web_highway.pdf) [S25]. If a western disturbance, dense fog or a frost warning is up, delay the start or the whole trip.

- Saproon (Pearl Spawn Lab) is in Solan town — a short local ride; exact premises **Unverified** until called.

## 4. Late-November weather in Solan

Use these as typical figures, then check the forecast before leaving.

**IMD station normals for Solan** (climatological tables in the Himachal climate publication; temperature normals are generally 1961–1990 in that volume) [S24]:

- November mean daily maximum **24.7°C**, mean daily minimum **5.6°C**.
- Solan district November rainfall **8.9 mm**, about **0.7 rainy days** (1951–2000 district normals). It is a dry month.
- October and November are described as the least-clouded months. Winter is counted from November to March.
- Frost: in the cold season, cold waves can take the minimum to about freezing or a little below, and frosts occur. The lowest temperature ever listed for the Solan observatory in that volume is **−3.0°C on 17 January 2000**, which is mid-winter, not November. Late-November frost is possible on a cold, clear night, especially in valleys and on shaded road cuts, but it is not the normal every-night condition.
- Fog: occasional in the cold season, particularly in valleys.

**UHF Nauni’s own campus note** (Nauni is 14 km away and a little lower, at 1,275 m): winter (November–February) minima about **−0.5°C to 9°C**, maxima about **16–23°C** [S12].

**Independent climate-summary sites** (modelled, not a substitute for IMD): Climate-Data.org gives a November average of 13.8°C, maximum 19.9°C, minimum 8.6°C, about 13 mm of rain and two wet days (1991–2021) [S26]. Weather Spark, using a MERRA-2 reanalysis and noting that Solan is far from a “reliable” station in their network, shows highs falling from about 22°C to 18°C and lows from about 9°C to 6°C through the month, with only about a 2% chance of a wet day [S11]. The IMD station maximum is warmer than these model pages; prefer IMD for planning, and still take a warm layer for a 5–6°C dawn ride.

**Practical implication:** days can be pleasant in the sun; nights and early-morning rides will feel cold, especially after Parwanoo and at Nauni. Pack for a 5°C start, not for a 24°C afternoon.

## 8. Field-data template (baseline questionnaire)

Use one sheet per grower or unit. Fill what the person is willing to share. Mark estimates clearly. The aim is a **comparable baseline for Kurukshetra (Thanesar and Pehowa)**, not a perfect census.

**Interview header**

- Date and time; place (village / town, tehsil, district); GPS or Maps pin if agreed
- Interviewer: Vipul Anand
- Respondent name, role (owner / family / manager / worker), phone (if they agree)
- How this meeting was arranged (DMR / state project / KVK / direct call)
- Consent: may the name and farm be cited in the study? May photographs be used? Yes / no / only anonymously

**A. Unit profile**

- Year started; why they started (training institute, family, neighbour, subsidy)
- Species grown now, and any dropped: white button (*Agaricus bisporus*); oyster / dhingri (*Pleurotus*); milky; shiitake; others
- Seasonal only, or year-round with cooling / AC? Number of rooms or huts, and approximate size
- Scale: bags or trays per cycle; kg per cycle; cycles per year; kg or tonnes per year (ask which unit they actually use)
- Land used (own / leased); how much is composting yard vs growing rooms

**B. Inputs**

- Compost: own pasteurisation, state unit at Chambaghat, a private supplier, or a mix? Price per quintal or tonne, and how far it travels
- Spawn: species / strain, supplier (DMR, UHF Nauni, Pearl, Vikas, Thakur, other), price per kg, booking lead time, quality problems if any
- Casing, bags, racks, energy (electricity bill in season), water, packaging
- Labour: family vs hired; men / women; number of people in a peak week; daily or monthly wage if they will say
- Any subsidy, training stipend, or bank loan actually received (scheme name if known)

**C. Output and money**

- Yield: kg mushroom per 100 kg compost, or per bag, for the last full cycle (and a good vs bad cycle)
- Selling price realised, not asking price: current, last winter, last summer, if they remember
- Buyers: local retail, Solan / Chandigarh mandi, Delhi (Azadpur or other), processors / canners, hotels, farm-gate
- Who sets the price? Any glut months? Any processing or drying?
- Rough cost and return for one cycle, if they are willing (keep it as their figure, not a cleaned-up model)

**D. Season and problems**

- Calendar: when they fill rooms, first flush, last flush
- Main problems this year: spawn, compost, temperature, disease or pests, power, labour, transport, price, water, regulation
- What they would change if they were starting again

**E. Comparison prompts for Kurukshetra**

- Could this method work in the Haryana plains in a seasonal hut, or only in an AC room?
- What raw material do they use for compost (wheat straw, paddy straw, poultry manure, other), and would Haryana’s wheat–paddy residue change the recipe?
- How far is their market, and what would change if the farm were in Pehowa or Thanesar instead?
- What training or after-care from DMR / UHF / the state project actually helped?
- Would they be willing to take a follow-up call, or to host a later visit by a Kurukshetra grower?

**F. After the meeting (interviewer notes)**

- What was seen (shed, compost yard, packing) vs only told
- Photographs taken, and consent noted
- Samples offered? Yes / no
- Open questions to check against the Haryana study
- Reliability: first-hand / estimated / overheard
